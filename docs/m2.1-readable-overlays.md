# M2.1 readable spatial overlays

M2.1 improves the readability of burned-in Japanese text while preserving the reviewed
temporal tracks and normalized OCR positions. It replaces the M2 single-line shrink fallback
with a bounded, auditable layout policy.

## Problem measured

The reviewed `0.mp4` timeline contains 207 approved translated regions. Of those source OCR
boxes, 197 are at most 32 pixels high and 129 are at most 24 pixels high. Several 80-102
character Japanese translations were therefore reduced to the former 10-pixel fallback to
remain on one line.

The M2.1 goal is not to redesign the slide. It is to keep spatial association with the source
while preventing unreadably small burned-in text.

## Layout policy

The `readable-spatial-v1` policy is deterministic:

1. Derive a resolution-scaled minimum font size (16 pixels at 1080p).
2. Try a single line in the original OCR width, reducing only to that minimum.
3. If it still does not fit, allow bounded horizontal expansion: at most 1.5 times the OCR
   width, at most 45 percent of the frame, and never beyond the frame edge.
4. If a readable single line is still impossible, wrap mixed Japanese and technical English
   into at most three lines before using an unrestricted fallback.
5. Keep ASCII technical tokens together where possible and avoid starting a line with closing
   Japanese punctuation.
6. Expand the dark replacement box to contain the readable text while still covering the
   original OCR box.
7. Resolve overlapping translated text boxes from top to bottom with a two-pixel gap when
   there is frame space.

The generated ASS file records `LayoutMode: readable-spatial-v1`. Render audit reports use
schema version 2 and include multiline, expanded, repositioned, minimum-font, and maximum-font
metrics. Media encoding, audio stream copy, atomic publication, overwrite guards, and source
fingerprint checks remain unchanged from M2.

## Remaining limitation and next target

M2.1 does not change scene detection or track geometry. Subsequent M2.2 diagnosis showed that
the specific 140-second drift was not evidence of undetected smooth scrolling: the prior
FFmpeg batch sampler declared 140.000 seconds while selecting the frame at approximately
140.500 seconds. M2.2 corrects the sampling policy and invalidates stale frame analyses. True
within-scene motion remains a possible limitation, but it was not demonstrated by this sample.
See `docs/m2.2-exact-timestamp-sampling.md` for the corrected diagnosis.

This remains spatial overlay replacement rather than generative inpainting. Burned-in text
cannot be toggled, narration is not translated, and configured excluded regions remain
unchanged. Generated media, ASS, reports, and QA frames remain ignored by Git.
